# Final 65 Checkpoint and Age Model Decision

**Status:** Engineering/data-model recommendation only. The final candidate set is non-production; clinical validation is false. This document does not change the candidate JSON, scoring, API, engine, or frontend.

## 1. Current engine assumptions

The current production engine reads `backend/app/config/milestones.json` (catalog version `draft-1`); it does not read `milestones_final_candidate.json`. In `app/services/milestone_service.py`, `milestones_for_age(age_months)` gathers integer `age_months` values, chooses the greatest checkpoint less than or equal to the supplied age, and returns every catalog item whose age equals that checkpoint. If the child is younger than the youngest configured checkpoint, it still returns the youngest checkpoint. Current configured ages are 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24, 36, 48, 60, and 72 months.

Age comes from date of birth as completed calendar months (`age_months` in `app/routers/children.py`). The child-specific milestone endpoint computes current age from DOB and returns `current_age_months`, `checkpoint_age_months`, `dataset_version`, and the selected items. `GET /milestones-age_months=...` uses the same floor-to-lower-checkpoint function.

`POST /screenings` recalculates the child age and expected set on the server. It rejects a stale/mismatched `checkpoint_age_months` or `milestone_dataset_version` with HTTP 409. It then requires each expected milestone ID exactly once: missing, unknown/out-of-checkpoint, and duplicate IDs are rejected by `validate_checkpoint_answers`. Answers are `YES`, `NO`, or `UNSURE`. The payload has one integer checkpoint age and a dataset version, but no age-band or source-age-basis field.

The exact selected question set is therefore the entire catalog subset at one chosen integer checkpoint, not a client-selected subset. Scores use the answer IDs to look up item domains and weights in the production catalog. Screening history stores the checkpoint, dataset version, answers, and a text/domain snapshot. The current output is thus structurally a single checkpoint, single item-set screening.

### Mismatch in the candidate age metadata

The final candidate JSON currently assigns `source_checkpoint` to all 26 AAP motor records even though its attached AAP review notes say the ages are mean ages for surveillance, not checkpoints or cutoffs. Conversely, its 39 CDC records have `months: null` and `age_basis: review_required`, although the evidence ledger and source references identify age-specific CDC pages. Three motor records also have WHO attainment ranges in source metadata; a single `months` integer cannot represent those ranges faithfully. These are candidate metadata semantics only; no values are changed here.

### Classification totals and reviewer decisions

- `CHECKPOINT_SUPPORTED`: 36 candidates. Their source references name exact CDC age pages. After content/domain decisions, their cited source age can be represented as that age-s source checkpoint; it must not be presented as clinical screening validation or a standalone cutoff.
- `AGE_BAND_REQUIRED`: 3 candidates: `gm_aap13_06_sit_unsupported` 3.8-9.2 months; `gm_aap13_12_stand` 6.9-16.9 months; `gm_aap13_12_walk` 8.2-17.6 months. These are WHO 1st-99th percentile attainment-window bounds. A future model must store lower and upper bounds as a range and label the range as attainment evidence. Do not select the midpoint, round bounds, or treat either bound as a clinical fail/pass age.
- `AGE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`: 23 AAP-only candidates. A qualified developmental/clinical reviewer must decide whether a reported mean surveillance age can be used as a question presentation checkpoint in SPARSH, and what population, visit timing, question administration and interpretation safeguards would be needed. Until approved, it is not a screening cutoff and should not be silently loaded as an integer checkpoint.
- `REMOVE_PENDING_REVIEW`: 3 candidates: `co_12_01`, `co_36_01`, and `co_36_03`. Their source ages remain identified in the table; the classification reflects the recommended content hold, not missing age evidence.

### What professional reviewers must decide

For all retained CDC items, confirm that the age-specific monitoring item is appropriate for the intended SPARSH use and population, including how -by age- milestone language is framed. For AAP items, decide explicitly whether mean surveillance ages may be used as checkpoint labels or whether they must remain source metadata only. For WHO-window items, decide whether and how an attainment window may be shown to staff without implying a screening threshold. Reviewers must also approve domain mapping, question wording, administration opportunities, response interpretation, referral language and any thresholds before production.

## 5. Hot-object safety decision

`co_36_03` asks whether a child avoids touching a hot object after being warned. The unsafe interpretation is to create or approach a heat hazard to elicit a response; an AWW must never be asked to stage that activity. Recommendation: **REMOVE** from the current AWW-administered question set, pending professional review. No risky activity is proposed. If later review considers a wording alternative, it must rely only on caregiver report or an already occurring observation and must not encourage exposure to heat.

## 6. Compare age-model options

| Option | Backend compatibility | Content fidelity | Implementation effort | Risk of misleading age interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Fixed age checkpoints** | Directly matches integer `age_months`, floor selection, and exact checkpoint answer IDs. The engine can represent the 36 surviving CDC records at source-listed ages without a new age type. | Good for CDC age-specific source pages if clearly labeled as monitoring ages. Poor for AAP mean ages if treated as thresholds; cannot represent WHO ranges. | Lowest for the age selector, though content-set rebuild, versioning and clinical approval are still needed. | High if mean ages are treated as expected-at-age cutoffs, if younger children inherit the first checkpoint, or if uneven question coverage is presented as comparable screening. |
| **B. Age bands / developmental windows** | Not compatible as-is: integer checkpoint selector and submit payload cannot express lower/upper range applicability; validator expects one exact set at one checkpoint. | Best for faithfully recording WHO attainment windows; cannot turn AAP means into bands without added evidence. CDC source-listed ages would still need a policy for representing discrete age-specific milestones. | High: age-band schema, band selection, request/response/history fields, overlap/tie rules, and answer-set validation need redesign. | Medium to high: a broad attainment window can be mistaken for an individual pass/fail interval unless explicitly framed and professionally approved. |
| **C. Hybrid** | CDC fixed checkpoints fit existing integer model. WHO ranges and unresolved AAP mean ages do not; a compatibility layer or deliberate hold is needed. | Best match to actual evidence types: retain CDC listed ages, represent WHO bounds as ranges, and keep AAP means as unassigned pending review. | Medium to high: add explicit age evidence types and band support, plus approval gates and exact-set versioning. | Lowest of the options if source age, age applicability, and screening decision thresholds remain distinct and AAP/WHO records are not coerced to checkpoints. |

## 7. Recommended model

**Recommend Option C, implemented as a staged hybrid with a strict hold on unresolved evidence.** Represent the 36 retained CDC items at their exact source-listed ages as discrete source checkpoints, after professional approval for SPARSH use. Keep the three WHO-backed records as WHO attainment ranges with their exact source bounds, never midpointed or rounded. Leave the 23 AAP-mean-only records unassigned to a SPARSH checkpoint until a professional reviewer explicitly approves an age interpretation. Keep the two overlap records and hot-object item excluded pending the decisions above.

This recommendation is a data-model direction, not approval to activate the 36 CDC items. The 65-item proposal should not be described as a complete multi-domain screen by itself: source-age distribution is uneven and removal decisions reduce domain counts. A professional must decide whether partial per-checkpoint coverage is acceptable or whether a content bank with a separately validated set per age is needed. There is no evidence here to add balancing items.

Do not use AAP means as cutoffs, transform WHO windows into exact ages, or label any selected age as clinical validation. Keep child chronological age, source age basis, applicability range, and any future screening decision threshold as separate concepts.

## 8. Exact backend changes that would eventually be required

No changes are made in this phase. If Option C is approved, the later implementation would need at least:

1. A versioned candidate/production catalog schema with distinct fields for `age_basis` (`cdc_source_checkpoint`, `aap_surveillance_mean`, `who_attainment_range`, `review_required`), source age, and applicability. For WHO, store exact `min_months`/`max_months` plus range type and percentile meaning; do not overload a single `age_months` field.
2. Replace or extend `milestones_for_age` so it selects by approved checkpoint or explicitly defined band, rather than always flooring chronological age to one integer. Define younger-than-first-checkpoint behavior explicitly; do not silently assign a younger child to a later question set.
3. A screening-set manifest per dataset version that defines the exact approved candidate IDs for each checkpoint/band, intended domains, and whether the set is complete enough for its stated use. Avoid automatically including all records whose numeric age happens to match.
4. API request/response model fields for a stable `screening_set_id` and age basis/range when applicable. The current `checkpoint_age_months` integer alone cannot identify an age band. Keep server-side recomputation of child age and reject stale set/version submissions.
5. Update answer validation to compare submitted IDs to the server-selected set for that set ID/version, still rejecting missing, unknown, and duplicate IDs. Persist the set ID, chronological age at screening, source-age metadata, dataset version, selected IDs and text snapshot in history for reproducibility.
6. Review domain aggregation and scoring against uneven set sizes, partial domains, and bands before use. This requires separately authorized scoring/clinical work; no weights or thresholds are proposed here.
7. Add migration/compatibility handling for existing screening histories and version changes; old `draft-1` records must remain interpretable under their stored version.
8. Update API contracts, frontend set-loading and answer submission, and tests together only after the schema and clinical/content policy are approved. This step would require explicit authorization in a later phase.

## 9. Professional approvals required before production

- A qualified developmental/clinical panel must approve the intended use and each retained source concept, item wording, domain placement, age interpretation, response opportunity and follow-up/referral handling; evidence provenance alone is not validation.
- Approve whether CDC age-specific milestone pages can be used as SPARSH presentation checkpoints and how the 75%-by-age monitoring framing is communicated.
- Decide the 23 AAP mean-age items individually or by justified policy; approve no automatic conversion to cutoffs.
- Approve representation/communication of the three WHO attainment windows and how their percentile bounds must not be used as a pass/fail rule.
- Resolve the two cross-domain overlap items and confirm whether explicit AAP subdomain mapping should take precedence. Review the resulting domain coverage; do not replace removed content with invented milestones.
- Confirm removal of `co_36_03`; prohibit any heat-exposure test.
- Approve wording/localization for AWW comprehension, local-language administration, and how YES/NO/UNSURE should be interpreted when the behavior was not observed.
- Separately validate the complete age-specific set, scoring, thresholds, red flags, recommendations and referral flow in the intended population before any production use.
